Structure, operation and switching behaviour of NMOS and PMOS, with practice and a quiz.

1. What is a MOSFET?

MOSFET = Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor. A voltage on the gate controls the current between drain and source. It is the building block of digital VLSI.

NMOS
Conducts with a high gate voltage. Carriers: electrons.
PMOS
Conducts with a low gate voltage. Carriers: holes.

4. PMOS Structure & Operation

PMOS is the mirror image: p+ source and drain in an n-type well. Its source is tied to VDD (5 V). Tap the diagram parts, then move the gate.

NMOS and PMOS work in opposite ways. That is why they are called complementary, and why CMOS uses them together.

5. MOSFET as a Switch

In digital circuits, logic 1 = VDD (high voltage) and logic 0 = 0 V (ground). The transistor acts as a switch controlled by the gate input A.

Input A: A = 0
NMOS switch (connects Y to GND)
OPEN
PMOS switch (connects Y to VDD)
CLOSED
NMOS passes a good 0. PMOS passes a good 1. This is why CMOS uses NMOS at the bottom (to ground) and PMOS at the top (to VDD).

7. Three Operating Regions – Basic Introduction

For an NMOS with VTH = 1 V. Move both sliders and see which region you are in.

VGS = 0.0 V
VDS = 0.0 V
Cutoff
VGS < VTH
No channel. Switch OFF.
Linear (Triode)
VGS > VTH and VDS < VGS − VTH
Channel complete. Acts like a resistor. Digital ON state.
Saturation
VGS > VTH and VDS ≥ VGS − VTH
Channel pinches off near the drain. Current stays almost constant. Used in amplifiers.
