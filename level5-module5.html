Design for Testability and Scan

Some circuits are almost impossible to test from their pins. DFT adds small hardware so every flip-flop can be set and read directly.

2

Why design for testability?

ATPG works well on combinational logic. Real chips are sequential: their behaviour depends on values stored in flip-flops. To test a fault deep inside, the tester must first drive the flip-flops into a particular state, which may take many clock cycles, and then wait more cycles for the effect to reach an output.

Imagine testing bit 15 of a 16-bit counter: you must clock it more than 32,000 times just to set that bit. Design for testability (DFT) adds a little extra hardware so every flip-flop can be set and read directly, turning a hard sequential problem into an easy combinational one.

3

Measuring testability: SCOAP

SCOAP (Sandia Controllability/Observability Analysis Program) puts numbers on the ideas from Module 1. A bigger number means more effort.

  • CC0 / CC1: effort to set a line to 0 / 1. Primary inputs have CC0 = CC1 = 1.
  • CO: effort to observe a line. Primary outputs have CO = 0.

Ad hoc DFT: test points

The simplest fix is a test point. An observation point connects a hard-to-observe line to an extra output. A control point adds a gate and a test input to force a hard-to-control line to 0 or 1. Compare the SCOAP numbers of n1 before and after adding an observation point.

Other ad hoc techniques: add a reset to every flip-flop, break long counters into smaller pieces for testing, and avoid logic that feeds back without passing through a flip-flop. Test points cost extra pins and area, so they are placed only where SCOAP says they help most.
5

The scan flip-flop

Scan design is the structured DFT method used in almost every digital chip. Each flip-flop is replaced by a scan flip-flop: a normal D flip-flop with a 2-to-1 multiplexer in front of it.

  • SE = 0 (normal / capture): the flip-flop loads D from the logic.
  • SE = 1 (shift): the flip-flop loads SI, the scan input, from the previous flip-flop.
6

Operating a scan chain

Connecting every Q to the next SI forms a scan chain, a long shift register. In shift mode the tester can load any state into every flip-flop, and read every flip-flop out through the single SO pin.

Scan test time and overhead

With one chain of length L and P patterns, each pattern needs L shift clocks plus 1 capture clock. Shifting out one response overlaps with shifting in the next pattern, so:

Test clocks ≈ (P + 1) × L + P

Splitting the flip-flops into k shorter chains, each with its own SI and SO pins, divides L by k.

What you learned

  • Sequential circuits are hard to test because internal states must be reached and observed through many clock cycles.
  • SCOAP gives CC0, CC1 and CO numbers; larger means harder.
  • Observation and control points are simple ad hoc fixes for hard-to-test lines.
  • A scan flip-flop is a D flip-flop with a multiplexer controlled by scan enable (SE).
  • In shift mode the flip-flops form a shift register, so every state can be loaded and read.
  • A scan test is: shift in, capture one clock, shift out (overlapped with the next shift in).
  • Test time grows with chain length; multiple shorter chains reduce it.
  • Full scan makes ATPG combinational at a cost of area and a small delay penalty.
